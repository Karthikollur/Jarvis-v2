# Chess club handbook

North Wharf Chess Club meets on the first Thursday of the month. The hall is booked under the word Fenwicket, which is written on the invoice and on the door card. Annual dues are 18 dollars, paid to the treasurer at the first meeting of the year. A guest may play one game free and then sits out. Sets and clocks live in the green crate under the window.

The chair opens the meeting, reads the minutes, and starts the first board. A counted vote settles a tie about pairings. Tea is poured after the second round, and the paper minutes stay in the folder on the side table. The door card is turned face out before anyone arrives so late members can see the booking word without asking at the desk.

Clocks are wound while the crate is still on the floor, then carried to the boards two at a time. A set with a missing piece is left in the crate with a note on the lid, and that board uses a spare pawn from the envelope taped inside the lid. The treasurer marks the dues book in pencil and writes a receipt on the spot. A guest who has used the free game may watch the rest of the evening from the side chairs.

The hall key goes back to the hook by the fire cupboard when the last board is packed. Tables are wiped, chairs are stacked in the marked corner, and the green crate is slid under the window with the lid latched. The folder of minutes is closed only after the result of the counted vote, if there was one, has been copied in. Lights are switched off at the door, and the invoice copy stays in the folder for the next booking.
