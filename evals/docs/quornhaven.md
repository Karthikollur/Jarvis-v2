# Wall herb log

The Wall Pocket is the narrow herb bed under the kitchen window. The first basil picking was on 12 June, a small bunch cut above the second pair of leaves. Basil cuttings were taken on 2 May and stood in a jar until they rooted. Mint stays in its own pot because it runs. Watering is at dusk, one can, along the wall and not on the sill. The soil is leaf mould with a little sharp sand.

Cuttings that fail are composted on the heap by the shed, not left to sour in the jar. The mint pot is turned a quarter turn when it starts to lean, so the stems do not reach out over the path. A paper tag marks the basil and is rewritten when the ink fades. The dusk can is filled from the courtyard butt, then carried in and poured slowly so the sill stays dry.

The window keeps the hardest afternoon sun off the leaves. Any leaf that touches the glass is tucked back before it scorches. A rooted cutting moves to its own corner of the pocket once the jar water stays clear. Mint roots that escape through the pot's base are lifted the same evening and put back before they meet the basil. The old paper tag goes to the compost with the failed cuttings, and the new tag is tied on the same day.
