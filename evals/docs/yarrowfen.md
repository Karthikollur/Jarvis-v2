# Coastal trip plan

The coastal trip leaves by ferry on 4 August from the west slip. Two nights are booked at Gull Loft, a small inn above the harbour path. Pack a wool layer and a dry bag. The map stays in the front pocket. Return is on the afternoon ferry two days later. The gate code for the inn is written on the paper booking, which stays in the bag.

If rain starts, the wool layer goes on before the walk down to the slip. The dry bag holds the map so a splash on the deck does not soak the paper. Supper is at the inn on both evenings, and breakfast is included, so the bag does not need a stove. The booking paper is checked again at the house door, then tucked into the inner pocket where the gate code can be read without unpacking.

The west slip has a covered bench if the ferry is late. Tickets are bought at the kiosk on the slip, not on the boat. The harbour path up to the inn is steep and has a handrail on the seaward side. Rooms look over the water, and the inn asks that wet coats be left on the pegs inside the door rather than carried upstairs. The return ferry is the mid-afternoon sailing, so the room is cleared before lunch and the key is left at the desk.

A spare wool layer stays at the bottom of the bag in case the evening wind picks up on the path. The map's coastal panel is the only one needed, and the inland panels can stay folded. If the kiosk queue is long, the booking paper is enough to show the inn's name while waiting. The dry bag is emptied on the kitchen table at home so damp paper is found the same day, and the gate code is not left written on a loose scrap.
