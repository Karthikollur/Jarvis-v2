# Courtyard vegetable log

The Long Crate bed is the sunny strip along the south wall. Tomatoes were set out on 7 March, variety Sunladder, one plant beside each cane. Watering is every third morning, two full cans, poured at the roots and not over the leaves. The soil mix is leaf mould and sharp sand, refreshed when the top looks pale. A cane tower was tied the same morning the plants went in. Gloves stay on the nail by the gate, and the paper notebook stays in the tin under the bench.

The weekly check looks at yellow leaves, loose ties, and whether the cans are full before the round starts. Empty cans are filled from the butt beside the shed. No spray is used. Ripe fruit is picked into the blue bowl and eaten the same day. Flower seedlings are lifted out so the fruit stays easy to reach.

After a dry week the leaf mould is scratched back over the roots and the sharp sand is left on top. A tie that has slipped down a cane is moved up one notch, and the tower is checked for lean toward the wall. The notebook gets one line for fruit picked and one line for any cane that needed a new tie. The blue bowl is rinsed at the outdoor tap and set back on the bench before the gate is latched.

On a cool morning the cans stand in the sun for a short while so the water is not icy on the roots. If the butt is low, the second can waits for the next round rather than being filled from the kitchen tap. Pale soil gets a fresh handful of leaf mould, not a deep dig. The south wall holds the day's heat, so the strip is watched for leaves that curl in the afternoon and recover by dusk.

Visitors are told the fruit still on the vine is for the house. Fallen fruit is cleared the same day so it does not sit on the sand and draw flies. The nail by the gate is the only home for the gloves, and the tin under the bench is the only home for the notebook. A loose cane is retied before the next watering even when the rest of the tower looks straight. The shed door is shut after the cans are refilled, and the latch is checked by hand.
